How it works in Michigan

  • Your funeral representative comes before your spouse and family. It's the only way an unmarried partner or chosen family gets the legal right to decide.
  • Who you can name: any adult of sound mind. Not an owner or employee of the funeral home, cemetery or crematory that will serve you, or a health professional or facility worker who cares for you in your last illness, unless they're your spouse or a relative.
  • How to sign it: in writing, dated and signed, with two witnesses or a notary, or both. You can also put it in your will or patient advocate designation instead of using this form.
  • If they can't do it: if they can't be found, resign, or don't act within 48 hours of being told of your death, the right passes to your successor, or if there isn't one, to your family in the order the law sets.
  • Changing your mind: sign a new designation, or a written revocation signed the same way.

If you don't name anyone

Michigan law decides, in this order: your spouse, then your adult children, grandchildren, parents, grandparents, siblings and other relatives. When several people share a rank, such as three adult children, a majority of those who can be reached decides.

What to do with it

  • Give a copy to your funeral representative and successor, and keep the original with your important papers.
  • Tell your family who you named, and why, while you can.
  • The funeral home can ask your representative for a copy, so make sure they can find it quickly.
  • This form says who decides, not what they decide. Write down what you want on the My celebration worksheet: graveandseed.com/handouts/my-celebration
