Someone died. The first few days.

A checklist for Washtenaw County, Michigan. You don't have to decide everything today.

Scan for the full guidesFuneral home prices, the first week, planning a gathering and more. graveandseed.com/resources

Right away

  • If they were in hospice, call the hospice's 24-hour line, not 911. A nurse comes, confirms the death and helps contact a funeral home.
  • If the death was sudden or unexpected, call 911. The county medical examiner may need to review it.
  • In a hospital, staff will guide you. Ask for the social worker or chaplain, take their belongings, and tell the nurse right away if they wanted to be an organ donor.
  • There's no rush once the death is confirmed. Take the time you need with them.

Choosing a funeral home

  • Michigan requires one, even for a simple cremation. A licensed funeral director moves the body, files the death certificate and gets the permits. They arrange the pickup from the hospital.
  • The least expensive choice is direct cremation, with no viewing or service at the funeral home. In Washtenaw County it costs {% include money.html n=p.homes.first.direct_cremation %} to {% include money.html n=p.homes.last.direct_cremation %} at funeral homes that post prices.
  • Call more than one. By law they must tell you prices over the phone. You only pay for what you choose, and embalming is rarely required.
  • A celebration of life can come later, weeks or months from now.

Have ready when you call

Their full legal name and Social Security number, place of birth, parents' names (including their mother's maiden name), years of school, military discharge papers if they served, and any prepaid funeral plan.

Who gets to decide

Michigan law sets the order: a funeral representative the person named in writing, then a spouse, adult children, grandchildren, parents, grandparents, siblings, and so on. Unmarried partners and chosen family aren't on the list unless they were named.

Who pays

  • Their bills, including the hospital's, are paid from their estate. Family members usually don't owe a relative's debts, unless they co-signed. Don't agree to pay a collector until you're sure.
  • Whoever signs the funeral home's contract agrees to pay it. Michigan law pays reasonable funeral costs from the estate before most other debts.
  • Help with costs: veterans' burial benefits from the VA, and Michigan's State Emergency Relief for families who can't afford a burial or cremation.

Death certificates

The funeral home files it and can order copies. Banks, insurers and pensions each want a certified copy, so order several at once. Washtenaw County Clerk: $15 for the first, $5 for each extra copy ordered together. 734-222-6720.

What can wait

The obituary, the estate, their car and taxes, and the celebration of life. Telling Social Security: the funeral home usually does it.

Free support

  • Arbor Hospice grief support, open to anyone: 734-709-3088
  • Ele's Place, grief groups for children, teens and their adults: 734-929-6640
  • If you're in crisis, call or text 988, any time.

Your rights

Under the federal Funeral Rule, a funeral home must give you its itemized price list when you visit and tell you prices over the phone. You can buy a casket or urn somewhere else, and they can't charge you for using it. You only have to buy the items you choose.
