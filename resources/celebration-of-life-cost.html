Most of the cost of a celebration of life is two things: the room and the food. Everything else is small by comparison. The budgets below are built from prices that Ann Arbor-area venues and caterers post publicly, checked in November 2026. Your numbers will differ, but these give you a place to start.

These are for the gathering itself. Cremation or burial is a separate cost, paid to a funeral home. Published direct cremation prices in Washtenaw County start at $895. See what funerals and cremation cost around Ann Arbor.

About 25 people at home: roughly $200 to $600

  • Place: a home or backyard, free.
  • Food: grocery deli trays, hot pans or a potluck with a few bought platters, about $5 to $15 a person, so $125 to $375.
  • Extras: printed photos, a guest book, flowers from the grocery store or a garden, borrowed chairs. A rough $50 to $200.

This is the most common kind of celebration, and often the warmest. The main cost is someone's time.

About 60 people in a rented room: roughly $850 to $3,300

  • Place: $150 to $1,000. Ann Arbor park shelters start around $55 for a weekday half day, library program rooms are $125 for four hours, the Ladies' Literary Club is $400 for the day, and Matthaei Botanical Gardens' auditorium is $1,000 for six hours. See venues with capacity and prices.
  • Food: $480 to $1,700. Restaurant trays run about $8 to $15 a person and boxed lunches $16 to $28. A staffed buffet is $40 to $60 a person or more. See food and local caterers.
  • Flowers, printing and rentals: $200 to $600. A rough estimate for a photo board, programs, flowers, and any tables, linens or a sound system the venue doesn't include.

For comparison, a memorial service in the chapel at Muehlig Funeral Chapel is listed at $1,295 on their price list. That covers the room and staff for the service, not food or a reception.

What changes the total most

  • The day of the week. Many venues charge less on weekdays, and a weekday afternoon is often easier for older guests anyway.
  • Waiting a few weeks. After a cremation there's no rush. More time means more venues to choose from and no rush fees from caterers.
  • Food style. Trays and drop-off cost a fraction of a staffed meal. Coffee and dessert in the afternoon costs less than lunch.
  • Nonprofit and resident rates. Several venues, like Matthaei and the Ladies' Literary Club, charge less for nonprofits or city residents. A congregation or club the person belonged to may host for free.
  • Asking for help. Friends who want to do something can bring a dish, set up chairs or make the photo board. People are usually glad to be asked.
