Name a funeral representative

Since 2016, Michigan lets any adult name someone to make decisions about their funeral, burial or cremation. That person comes before a spouse or family. It's the only way an unmarried partner or chosen family gets the legal right to decide.

  • Put it in writing, date it and sign it.
  • Have it either witnessed by two people or notarized. The person you're naming can't be a witness.
  • It can go in your will, your patient advocate designation, or its own document. A separate document keeps your will private.
  • You can name a backup. The person you name doesn't have to be a relative, but generally can't be someone from the funeral home or cemetery that will handle your arrangements.
  • Give copies to the person you named and keep one with your important papers.

Print our fill-in funeral representative form, with places for witnesses or a notary.

Michigan doesn't publish an official form, so any signed statement that meets these rules works. The law is MCL 700.3206 to 700.3206b.

Prepaid funeral contracts

Michigan law protects prepaid funeral plans. The money must be held in escrow, you get a yearly statement, and the seller must be registered with the state.

  • You can cancel within 10 business days of signing for a full refund.
  • After that, you can still cancel with 30 days' written notice and get 90% to 100% back, depending on the contract.
  • Questions or complaints: LARA at 517-241-7000, or the Attorney General's consumer line at 877-765-8388.

Prepaying isn't the only way to plan. Writing down what you want and setting money aside in a payable-on-death account can work too.

Advance directives

A patient advocate designation names someone to make medical decisions if you can't. Michigan requires two witnesses who aren't your close family, heirs, doctor or the advocate. Any form that follows Michigan's rules works at any hospital here, but your own health system's form can be easier to get into your chart. Free forms:

  • Michigan Medicine (U-M Health): the advance directives booklet has the form. Printed copies are at any U-M clinic or inpatient unit. More from U-M Health
  • Trinity Health (Ann Arbor and Chelsea hospitals): the Choosing My Patient Advocate form, also in Spanish. Send a copy to Trinity Health Ann Arbor's medical records (fax 734-712-7387) so it's on file. More from Trinity Health
  • IHA (Trinity's primary care practices): its own form, also in large print. Ask your IHA doctor to add it to your chart.
  • VA Ann Arbor: veterans can use VA Form 10-0137, which also covers a living will. The VA accepts Michigan forms too, and its social workers help fill them out.
  • Anyone: Michigan Legal Help's do-it-yourself health care power of attorney, another name for the same thing.

Do-not-resuscitate orders and MI-POST

A do-not-resuscitate order tells EMS and other responders not to start CPR if your heart and breathing stop outside a hospital. You sign it with your doctor and two adult witnesses, at least one of whom isn't your spouse, parent, child, grandchild, sibling or heir. Keep it where responders can find it at home, and tell your family where it is. You can also wear a DNR bracelet. The form is in Michigan Medicine's advance directives booklet (above), hospices have it, and the state's required wording is in MCL 333.1054. Inside a hospital, you decide about CPR with your care team instead.

MI-POST is a medical order for people with serious illness or frailty, filled out with your doctor, NP or PA. It covers CPR and other care in a crisis. It has to be the current state form: MDHHS-5836, with its patient and family information sheet. Both come in Spanish and Arabic too. More from the state

A simple will

Michigan law includes a fill-in-the-blanks statutory will. Signed in front of two adult witnesses, it's a valid will, no lawyer needed. It's limited: you can leave cash gifts to up to two people or charities, and the form decides how everything else goes to your spouse and children. If you want something different, a lawyer can write a custom will.

  • Fill it in online with Michigan Legal Help's free do-it-yourself will, which prints a statutory will. Their guide to making a will in Michigan explains each choice.
  • Or print it from the Michigan Legislature's free Planning for Your Peace of Mind booklet (updated April 2026), which also has a financial power of attorney form, plain explanations of patient advocates and funeral representatives, and pages for recording your accounts and funeral arrangements. The form is also in the law itself, MCL 700.2519.

A will may not be found or read until after the funeral, so keep your funeral representative and wishes somewhere your people can get to quickly.

Body donation

Medical schools like Wayne State, Michigan State, Oakland University and the University of Michigan accept whole-body donations, and some cover transport and cremation. You need to register ahead, and programs can still decline at the time of death, so have a backup plan. More on lower-cost options

Write down what you want

A funeral representative isn't legally bound by your wishes, so tell them clearly. A page is enough. Print the My celebration worksheet to fill in, or cover these:

  • Burial, cremation, green burial or donation
  • Where you'd like to end up
  • What kind of gathering, if any, and who should be there
  • Music, readings, food, the stories you want told
  • Where your important papers and any prepaid contract are kept
