People prepay so their family won't have to decide or pay at a hard time. That can work. But plans change. People move, choose cremation instead of burial, or need the money. How much you can get back depends on what kind of thing you bought, and one contract often mixes several kinds.

The short version: money for funeral and cemetery services is the best protected. Merchandise sold by a cemetery, like a vault or marker, is refundable until it's engraved or delivered. A plot, crypt or niche is not refundable under Michigan's prepaid law at all.

Three kinds of purchases, three sets of rules

1. Funeral home goods and services, and cemetery services

This covers things like the funeral director's services, a casket bought from the funeral home, embalming, and cemetery services such as cremation, opening and closing a grave, and installing a marker.

  • The money goes into escrow. Funds paid on a prepaid contract must be held by an escrow agent, not spent by the seller. The seller may add a commission of up to 10% of the price, which it keeps. MCL 328.222
  • You can cancel any time before the death, with 30 days' written notice. You get back at least 90% of what's in escrow, including interest, if no commission was charged; at least 95% if the commission was 5% or less; and 100% if it was more than 5%. MCL 328.223(1)

2. Merchandise sold by a cemetery

The law counts vaults and other outer burial containers, grave markers and monuments, and urns as cemetery merchandise.

  • Less is set aside. A cemetery may put as little as 80% of what you pay for merchandise into escrow. Before 2006 the minimum was 60%. MCL 328.222(2)
  • But the refund is larger. If you cancel before the death, the cemetery owes you 100% of what you paid, plus any rise in the Detroit consumer price index since you signed. You can cancel just the merchandise and keep the rest of the contract. MCL 328.223(2) and (5)
  • Once a marker is engraved, it's yours, not refundable. The law lists inscribed grave memorials among the things with no refund. Merchandise also stops being "prepaid" once it's actually delivered into your possession. A cemetery storing it for you doesn't count as delivery. Act 255 of 1986, sections 5 and 13(6)

3. Plots, crypts and niches

A grave, a crypt in a mausoleum, a niche for ashes and other "burial rights or other land interests" are not merchandise under the prepaid law. The law states plainly that no refunds are available for them. MCL 328.223(6)

  • Some cemeteries buy plots back. Many don't. You can usually sell or give a plot to someone else, but the transfer generally has to go through the cemetery's records, and it may charge a fee.
  • One exception: if you buy space in a mausoleum or columbarium that hasn't been built yet and it isn't finished in time, Michigan law requires the cemetery to offer a full refund with 4% yearly interest. MCL 456.536a

If you're not sure you'll be buried in that place, think hard before buying a plot ahead of time. It's the part of a prepaid plan that's hardest to undo.

Rights every prepaid contract must give you

  • A 10-business-day cooling-off period. You can revoke within 10 business days of signing and get back everything you paid. The contract has to say so right above where you sign. MCL 328.225(3)
  • The refund amount in writing. The contract must state your right to cancel and the percentage you'd get back. LARA bulletin on required contract language
  • A yearly statement. At least once a year you should get a statement showing the balance, any fees charged, what you'd get back if you canceled, and who holds the money. If you haven't been getting one, ask. MCL 328.222(14)
  • Notice if the business is sold. If the seller hands your contract to another company, including when more than half of the business changes hands within a year, you must be told in writing. You then have 30 days to cancel for a full refund, commission included. Plots, crypts and engraved markers are excluded. MCL 328.223(6)
  • A registered seller. Anyone selling prepaid funeral or cemetery contracts in Michigan must be registered with the state. A contract with an unregistered seller can be voided by the buyer. LARA's guide to the law · MCL 328.226

After the death

Once the person the contract is for has died, the contract generally can't be canceled. The exceptions: there are no remains, the remains can't be recovered, or the family didn't know the contract existed and made other arrangements. In those cases the refund is due within 30 days of asking. MCL 328.223(4) Tell your family where the contract is kept.

Older contracts

Michigan's prepaid law took effect July 1, 1987, and its escrow and refund rules were strengthened in 2005. The law doesn't spell out how it applies to contracts signed before 1987. If you or a parent have a contract from the 1970s or '80s, get a copy of it and ask the seller in writing for the escrow records. If the answer doesn't add up, call the state (below).

Federal rules don't cover most cemeteries

The FTC's Funeral Rule, which requires itemized price lists and lets you buy only what you want, applies to funeral homes. It does not apply to cemeteries without an on-site funeral home, or to monument sellers. FTC: the Funeral Rule For a cemetery, the contract and Michigan law are what protect you.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Which items on this contract are services, which are merchandise, and which are land (plot, crypt or niche)? What's the refund for each if I cancel?
  • Who holds the money, and will you send me proof of the deposit and a yearly statement?
  • Is the price guaranteed, or could my family owe more later?
  • What commission are you charging?
  • If I move or change my mind, can I transfer this to another funeral home or cemetery?
  • When will the marker be engraved? (Waiting keeps it refundable.)
  • Am I being rushed? A real plan will still be there next week.

The Michigan Attorney General suggests getting it in writing whether the contract is guaranteed, revocable, refundable, transferable and portable. AG consumer alert

If you want to cancel

  1. Find the contract and any statements. If you can't find them, ask the seller for copies.
  2. Send a dated letter, by certified mail, listing each item you want refunded. Keep a copy.
  3. Ask for the escrow records: how much was deposited, interest earned, fees, and how the refund was calculated.
  4. Check the refund against the three categories above. Plots and engraved markers won't be refunded; services and unengraved merchandise should be.
  5. If it still looks short, file a complaint with LARA, which regulates prepaid sellers and cemeteries: the Cemetery Commissioner is at 517-241-9221 or LARA-Cemetery@michigan.gov. You can also contact the Attorney General's consumer line at 877-765-8388. LARA: cemeteries

The law also lets a buyer sue in circuit court to enforce it, and a court can award attorney fees to the side that wins. MCL 328.234

Other ways to plan ahead

Prepaying isn't the only way to spare your family. You can write down what you want, name a funeral representative, and set money aside in a payable-on-death bank account, which stays yours until it's needed.
