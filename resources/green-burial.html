Green burial means the body goes into the ground in a way that lets it return to the soil: no embalming, a shroud or simple biodegradable container, and no concrete vault. Michigan law doesn't require embalming or a vault, but most cemeteries require a vault, and many places that say they "offer green burial" still do.

What to ask any cemetery

  • Is a vault or grave liner required? This is the big one. A shroud inside a concrete vault isn't a green burial.
  • Is embalming allowed or required?
  • What containers are allowed? Shroud, wicker, plain pine, cardboard.
  • What markers are allowed? Some allow flat stones, some allow nothing.
  • Are there religious or residency rules, and do residents pay less?

Hybrid cemeteries have a green section inside a regular cemetery. Natural burial grounds are fully green. Conservation burial grounds also protect the land; Michigan doesn't have one yet, though one is being planned near Jackson.

There is currently no true green burial inside Ann Arbor. The closest options are in Chelsea, Milan and Dexter. Details checked {{ g.updated }}. Rules and prices change, so always confirm with the cemetery.
