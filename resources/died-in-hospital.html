The hospital staff do this every day, and they'll walk you through the next few hours. Here's what to expect, so nothing surprises you.

Before you leave the room

  • You can take your time. Ask the nurse for as long as you need to sit with them, and for others to come in and say goodbye.
  • Ask for the chaplain or the social worker. Helping people right after a death is their job, whatever your beliefs.
  • Take their belongings with you. Staff usually pack them in a bag. If anything is missing later, call the hospital's security office.
  • If they wanted to be an organ or tissue donor, tell the nurse right away. Donation happens soon after death, and Gift of Life Michigan will contact you.
  • If they signed up to donate their body to science, the family still has to call the program after the death. The program decides then whether it can accept them.

What happens to their body

  • They'll go to the hospital morgue and stay there until a funeral home picks them up.
  • Staff will ask which funeral home you're using. It's fine not to know yet. Morgue space is limited, so ask how long you have to decide.
  • Michigan requires a funeral home, even for the simplest cremation. A licensed funeral director has to move the body, file the death certificate and get the cremation or burial permit. Hospitals don't arrange cremation themselves.
  • The funeral home does the rest. Once you choose one, they call the hospital and arrange the pickup. You don't have to.

Choosing a funeral home

  • The least expensive choice is direct cremation. Their body goes from the hospital morgue to the crematory, with no viewing or service at the funeral home. Around here it ranges from {% include money.html n=p.homes.first.direct_cremation %} to {% include money.html n=p.homes.last.direct_cremation %}. Compare every Washtenaw funeral home's posted prices
  • Call more than one. Funeral directors have to give you their prices over the phone. Comparing isn't disrespectful, and you only pay for what you choose.
  • Before you sign, check them out. See who owns each Washtenaw funeral home and whether its license is current.
  • Have this ready when you call: their full legal name and Social Security number, place of birth, their parents' names (including their mother's maiden name), years of school, military discharge papers if they served, and any prepaid funeral plan.
  • The celebration can come later. With direct cremation, there's no rush to plan a gathering. Many people hold one weeks or months afterward. How to plan a celebration of life

Autopsy and the medical examiner

  • The hospital may ask permission for an autopsy. You can say yes, no, or yes to a partial one. At Michigan Medicine there's no charge, it's done soon after death, and it doesn't delay a funeral or cremation. The final report can take up to 10 weeks.
  • If the death was sudden, unexpected or from an injury, the county medical examiner may take over, and an autopsy can be required by law. In Washtenaw County, the medical examiner's office is part of Michigan Medicine. You still choose the funeral home, and they pick up your person once the exam is done.

Who pays

  • Hospital bills are paid from their estate, not by you. Family members usually don't have to pay a relative's debts from their own money. If there isn't enough in the estate, the debt usually goes unpaid. There are exceptions, like a bill you co-signed or agreed to pay yourself. If a collector calls, don't agree to pay anything until you're sure you owe it.
  • Funeral costs come first. Whoever signs the funeral home's contract agrees to pay it. Michigan law then pays reasonable funeral and burial costs from the estate before hospital bills and most other debts.
  • Veterans: if they died while being cared for at the VA Ann Arbor hospital, the VA pays a burial allowance even when the death wasn't related to their service. VA burial allowance
  • If money is tight, Michigan's State Emergency Relief program can help pay for a burial or cremation. State burial help and other lower-cost options

At Trinity Health Ann Arbor and other hospitals

Trinity Health Ann Arbor used to be called St. Joseph Mercy Ann Arbor. At any hospital, ask your nurse for the social worker or chaplain. They can tell you the hospital's timeline for choosing a funeral home and who to call later with questions.

What can wait

  • Death certificates. The funeral home files the certificate and can order copies for you.
  • Telling Social Security, banks and everyone else. The first-week checklist covers who to tell and when.
  • The obituary. How to write one that sounds like them
  • The celebration of life. It can happen weeks or months from now. If you'd like help with it, that's what I do.
