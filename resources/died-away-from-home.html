When someone dies in another state, or dies here and will be buried somewhere else, getting them where they need to go can be the biggest bill of all. It's also one most families don't see coming. Here's how it works and where the money goes.

Moving a body

Moving a body usually takes two funeral homes. The one near the death forwards the body, and the one at the other end receives it. Each charges a fee, and both fees have to be on their price lists.

For example, Muehlig Funeral Chapel in Ann Arbor lists:

  • Forwarding remains to another funeral home: $3,695
  • Receiving remains from another funeral home: $2,395
  • Taking the body to or from the airport: $395 within 35 miles, plus $4 a mile beyond that
  • An air tray, the wood-and-cardboard shipping case: $195

From their price list, effective October 1, 2026. Other funeral homes charge less; compare prices.

The airline charges on top of that. Delta, the main airline at Detroit Metro, posts its rates for shipping a body inside the U.S.: about $490 to $1,200 for an adult in a casket, depending on where it's going. Their rate sheet is from 2021, so expect more today, plus a screening fee and taxes. Most other airlines don't post their prices. Delta Cargo rate sheet

Families can't book this themselves. Only a funeral home the airline has approved as a "known shipper" can send a body by air. The body travels as cargo, not with the family.

Michigan only requires embalming for shipping if the body won't arrive within 48 hours of the death. More about embalming

Moving ashes instead

Often the simplest choice is to have a direct cremation near where the person died, then bring the ashes home. Direct cremation in Washtenaw County starts at $895, and prices in other places are often similar. You skip the forwarding fee, the receiving fee and the airline. The celebration can happen later, wherever the people are.

This isn't right for everyone. If your family or faith needs a burial or a viewing with the body, moving the body is worth the cost. Just ask both funeral homes for their fees before you agree to anything.

What it adds up to

Say someone dies in Ann Arbor and will be buried in another state. Here's a rough all-in cost for each way, using posted prices. Muehlig is one of the higher-priced funeral homes here, so it shows close to the top of the range.

Flying the body: about $7,200 to $7,900

  • Forwarding the body: $3,695
  • Airport trip and shipping case: $590
  • Airline: about $490 to $1,200, plus a screening fee
  • Receiving it at the other end: $2,395 at Muehlig's price; ask the funeral home there for theirs

Cremating here and mailing the ashes: about $950 to $4,400

  • Direct cremation: $895 at the lowest posted price in Washtenaw County, or $4,160 at Muehlig
  • Death certificates: $15 for the first, $5 for each extra
  • Mailing: about $40 to $100 in postage if you do it yourself, or $150 plus postage if Muehlig does it

Neither total includes what happens at the other end: the grave, the burial or a place for the ashes. Funeral homes and airlines can add fees that aren't posted anywhere, so ask each one for a written total before you agree.

Mailing ashes

  • Only the U.S. Postal Service will ship ashes. FedEx, UPS and DHL won't take them.
  • You have to use Priority Mail Express. That's true inside the U.S. and abroad, where it's Priority Mail Express International.
  • You have to use the Postal Service's own box. Since March 2025, ashes must ship in the Priority Mail Express Cremated Remains box. You can't use your own box anymore. The box comes in a free kit from the USPS Postal Store (search "cremated remains kit"). It's mailed to you, so order it a few days ahead.
  • The label is part of the box. Every side, top and bottom included, is printed with the bright orange "Cremated Remains" label, called Label 139. Postal workers can see what it is from any angle, and it gets handled with care.
  • Pack it well. Put the ashes in a sealed container that won't leak fine dust, like an urn or the plastic box from the crematory. Put that in a plastic bag, then cushion it in the box. Tuck a slip inside with both names and addresses in case the outside label comes off.
  • Cost: postage for an adult's ashes usually runs about $40 to $100, depending on weight and distance. Many funeral homes will mail them for you for a fee. Muehlig charges $150, plus the postage.
  • Sending to a cemetery? Call first and make arrangements. Don't just mail ashes and hope they know what to do.
  • Sending abroad? Some countries don't accept ashes by mail. Check the Postal Service's rules for that country first. USPS: How to Package and Ship Cremated Remains

Flying with ashes

  • Carry them on. Airport security allows ashes in a carry-on or a checked bag, but a carry-on keeps them with you. Some airlines, like Southwest and United, only allow them as a carry-on.
  • Use a container the X-ray can see through, like plastic or wood. Metal and stone urns often can't be cleared. Security officers will not open the container, even if you ask. If they can't see inside, it can't go on the plane. Travel with the crematory's plastic box and move the ashes into a nicer urn once you're home.
  • Bring paperwork. Carry the cremation certificate and a copy of the death certificate. Check your airline's rules before you book.

Questions to ask

  • What is your fee to forward remains? What does the other funeral home charge to receive them?
  • What's the total, including airport trips, the shipping case and the airline?
  • What would a direct cremation here cost instead, with the ashes mailed home?
  • If you're shipping by air: are you an approved known shipper with the airline?
