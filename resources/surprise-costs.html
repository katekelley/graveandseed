Most people expect the funeral bill. What surprises them is everything around it: fees that show up later, bills that arrive in the mail, and costs nobody mentioned. Here's what to watch for in Michigan, starting with the good news.

Their bills are the estate's, not yours

  • Medical bills, ambulance bills, credit cards and other debts are paid from what they left, not from family members' own money. If there isn't enough, those debts usually go unpaid.
  • The exceptions: a loan or card you co-signed, a joint account, or a bill you agreed in writing to pay yourself.
  • The funeral is different. Whoever signs the funeral home's contract agrees to pay it. Michigan law then lets the estate pay reasonable funeral and burial costs first, before most other debts.
  • If a collector calls, don't agree to pay anything or give a card number until you're sure you owe it. "I'm not responsible for this debt. Please send it to the estate in writing" is enough.

Extras on the funeral bill

  • When and where they died. Some funeral homes charge more for a death at night, on a holiday, or at home. Cole Funeral Chapel's price list, for example, adds $120 for a death at night and $60 for a death at home.
  • "Cash advance" items. These are things the funeral home pays for you and adds to the bill: death certificates, the medical examiner's cremation permit, clergy, musicians, flowers and the newspaper obituary. Ask whether they add a fee on top. They have to tell you if they do.
  • Ask for the itemized statement before you sign. Federal law says they must give you one that lists each item and its price.

Death certificates

Banks, insurers, pension plans, the Secretary of State and the probate court may each want their own certified copy. For deaths in Washtenaw County, the County Clerk charges $15 for the first copy and $5 for each extra copy ordered at the same time. Extra copies are cheapest when you order them together, so order several at once. Families often need 5 to 10.

Probate, and Michigan's inventory fee

If they left things in their name alone, like a house, a car or a bank account with no one named to inherit it, someone may need to go through probate court to settle it. The court fees surprise almost everyone.

  • Opening an estate: $150, plus a $25 electronic filing fee.
  • Letters of Authority, the paper banks want to see: $10 plus $1 a page for each certified copy.
  • The inventory fee. Michigan charges every probate estate a fee based on what the estate is worth, and the court can't waive it. A few examples:

Any mortgage or loan on a house is subtracted from its value first.

  • A notice to creditors, published in a newspaper, is usually required too, and the newspaper charges for it.
  • A lawyer, if you hire one, usually bills by the hour. Many families handle a simple estate themselves with free help from Michigan Legal Help.
  • Small estates cost less. If the estate is small enough, you may be able to skip full probate. See the small estate limit
  • Things that skip probate entirely: life insurance and retirement accounts with a named beneficiary, joint accounts, and bank accounts marked "payable on death."

Washtenaw County Probate Court: 101 E. Huron St., Ann Arbor, (734) 222-3072.

The empty house

  • The bills keep coming: mortgage, property taxes, utilities, internet, lawn care and snow removal.
  • Call the home insurer right away. Many homeowner policies cover less once a house sits empty for a while, often 30 or 60 days. The insurer can tell you what you need to keep it covered.
  • Keep the heat on in winter to stop pipes from freezing.
  • Clearing it out costs money too: a dumpster, movers, a storage unit, cleaners, or an estate sale company, which usually keeps a share of what sells. Take your time if you can. Most of this can wait.

Later costs

  • The headstone or marker. It's usually ordered months after the funeral. Cemeteries often charge separately to set it or pour its foundation, and some only pour foundations in warm months. Local cemetery prices, including marker fees
  • The obituary, if you publish one in the paper. It can cost $170 to more than $1,200. What local papers charge, and free options
  • Taxes. A final income tax return is due on the usual schedule, and some estates need their own return. A tax preparer may charge more for these.
  • Travel and food for family coming in, and for the gathering.
