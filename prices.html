Embalming

Embalming is usually a policy, not the law

Michigan only requires embalming for a few contagious diseases, or when a body is shipped more than 48 hours after death (state rule). It doesn't protect public health. Many funeral homes still require it for a public visitation as their own policy, and every price list says it "may be necessary" for a viewing. Before you agree to it, ask:

  • Will you arrange a private family viewing without embalming?
  • Do you refrigerate instead, and what does that cost?

With a willing funeral director, you can also keep your person at home for a vigil of a day or two, kept cool with dry ice. More about embalming in Michigan, with sources

Your rights

Under the federal Funeral Rule, a funeral home must give you its itemized price list when you visit, and tell you prices over the phone. You can buy a casket or urn somewhere else, and they can't charge you for using it. You can buy only the items you want. More from the FTC
